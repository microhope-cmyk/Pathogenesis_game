About

The game developer is an Immunologist with a toddler and very little free time — but this game has been kicking around in her head for years with no way out. A recent Claude coding hobby has finally given it a means to escape. The goal is to make learning real immunology concepts fun without being too overwhelming.

If you'd like to show your support for further game development and toddler snack purchases, follow this link.

Support

This game is still in whatever comes before beta testing, so — if you have suggestions about gameplay, requests for additional features, or bugs to report, please email joy.us.games@gmail.com, but understand that the developer is not likely to resolve issues in a timely manner… especially if she's run out of Claude credits for the week.

Immunology is very complex. If you are a scientist with feedback about the science involved, please take a minute to think about the balance between accuracy and gameplay and how your suggestion impacts game mechanics.

Pathogenesis

Infection and immunity, fought one turn at a time.

Reading the stat bar

    Reading a card

      The idea

      Each side manages one host body. Play Pathogens to attack the opponent's Health; play Immune cells to defend your own. Bring the opponent's Health to zero to win.

      ATP, your resource

      Your ATP capacity rises by 1 each turn (up to 8) and refills. Every card costs ATP to play — the number in the blue circle. Once a card is on the board, using it also costs 1 ATP each time — attacking, engaging, or secreting all draw from the same pool as playing new cards, so a full board doesn't let you act for free forever. You draw 2 cards a turn, so there's usually something worth spending it on.

      Duration: quick vs. lasting

      Every card is one of two tempos. Pathogens are tagged Acute or Persistent; Immune cells are tagged Innate or Adaptive — same underlying rule, immunology's own names for it. The quick tempo (Acute / Innate) can act the instant it's played, but only once — after attacking, blocking, or engaging a single time, it's spent. The lasting tempo (Persistent / Adaptive) sits out its first turn — incubating, for a pathogen; resting, for a cell — then can act every turn, repeatedly, until destroyed.

      Attacking & blocking

      Tap a ready Pathogen to send it at the opponent's Health — this costs 1 ATP, so it's grayed out if you're out. They may block with a ready Immune cell — both take damage from each other — or take the hit. Blocking is free.

      Engaging

      Tap a ready Immune cell to proactively attack an enemy Pathogen already on the board — no blocking involved. This costs 1 ATP too. This is the only way to actually clear a chronic infection; blocking just absorbs one turn's damage.

      Specialization

      A Naive Helper T-cell or Cytotoxic T-cell permanently upgrades the first time it fights a Pathogen, becoming Specialized against that category and choosing one of two paths: Effector (a strong one-time Potency boost, held in reserve until its next fight) or Memory (+2 Resilience, forever). A Naive B-cell instead becomes a Memory B-cell (+2/+2, forever) or a Plasma Cell (secretes Antibodies each turn). Vaccination can Specialize a cell early, before it ever meets the real thing — it grants a flat +1/+1 with no path choice. Afterward, a Specialized cell deals +1 Potency any time it fights a Pathogen of its own category.

      Chronic complications

      A few Pathogens (HPV, EBV, H. pylori, Hepatitis C, Group A Strep) cause lasting harm if left unremoved for several of your turns — extra Health loss, or worse. Destroying the Pathogen is usually the only way to stop it.

      Effects

      One-shot cards — Vaccination, mutation, targeted treatment, Fever Response, Cytokine Storm. Antibiotics, antivirals, and antifungals only work on their matching Pathogen category.
